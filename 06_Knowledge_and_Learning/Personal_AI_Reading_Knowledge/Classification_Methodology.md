# Classification and Reading Prioritization Methodology

## 1. Objective

Organize a personal reading library into useful learning domains and identify a manageable shortlist for professional development.

Classification is a navigation aid, not a substitute for reading, expert evaluation or human review.

## 2. Two-stage approach

### Stage A — Broad thematic classification

Use available metadata such as title, author, category and publisher to assign one or more candidate themes.

The pilot's displayed subject groups included Finance and Accounting; AI and Technology; Controlling / FP&A; Investment and Wealth Management; Economics and Business Strategy; Data Analytics and BI; SAP / ERP and Enterprise Systems; and Other / To be classified.

### Stage B — Professional relevance prioritization

Review candidate books against learning themes such as Finance Transformation, SAP and enterprise systems, Controlling and FP&A, AI in Finance, data analytics and business intelligence, and management and leadership.

A shortlist should favor relevance to a defined learning objective rather than merely matching popular keywords.

## 3. Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metadata match | Candidate theme inferred from title or catalog metadata |
| Multi-theme match | Metadata suggests relevance to more than one theme |
| Human reviewed | User inspected the book and confirmed the classification |
| Content verified | Classification checked against actual book content |

Do not use “Content verified” unless the content was genuinely reviewed.

## 4. Confidence and ambiguity

A practical rubric for future iterations:
- **High:** title and available metadata strongly indicate the theme.
- **Medium:** title suggests the theme, but scope is broad or ambiguous.
- **Low:** only weak keyword overlap exists; manual review is required.

The pilot's existing classifications should not be described as content-verified. The workflow explicitly treated them as title/metadata-based.

## 5. Prioritization criteria

A future ranking can consider:
1. Relevance to the current learning goal.
2. Direct applicability to Finance, Controlling or transformation work.
3. Foundational value and conceptual depth.
4. Complementarity with books already studied.
5. Availability of personal notes to support review.
6. Feasibility of completing the book in the available time.

Keep score, rationale and human override visible. Do not imply the existing shortlist used a scoring model unless that model is implemented and documented.

## 6. Human-in-the-loop review

For each candidate: inspect title and metadata; read the description or contents where lawfully available; confirm or revise the theme; record why it matters; then decide whether to read, revisit, defer or exclude it.

## 7. Quality checks

- Every indexed item should retain a stable private source identifier.
- Avoid duplicate book records.
- Preserve record type when distinguishing albums/audiobooks from electronic books.
- Report missing author/title metadata.
- Keep source timestamps distinct from publication dates.
- Track unclassified records explicitly.
- Re-run validation after each index refresh.
